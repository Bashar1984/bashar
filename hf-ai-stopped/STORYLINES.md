# What if AI stopped working? Three storylines

The one thing: most AI works quietly inside ordinary tools, so if it stopped, life wouldn't end. It would get slower and noisier, and people would fill the gaps.

Real number used: 45.6% of email traffic was spam in 2023 (Kaspersky, Spam and phishing in 2023). Everything else is a thought experiment and is labelled as one on screen.

## A. One ordinary morning (recommended: a person and a situation everyone knows)

- **Device / form:** a moment in someone's day; one continuous take that travels from object to object.
- **What you see and feel:** Sam's ordinary 8:00 a.m. on a phone screen, where every small AI helper quietly fails, one after another. It's funny at first, then you notice how much was invisible.
- **First 2 s:** a phone lock screen reading `08:00` with three notifications. The camera pushes into the map app as a "Rerouting…" spinner freezes and goes grey.
- **Beats:** hook (frozen map) → inbox floods, and **45.6%** lands as a wall of spam → the card payment at the café is held "under review" → the translate button returns nothing → Sam looks up from the phone and asks a person / unfolds a paper map → claim: "We'd slow down. Not stop."
- **Look:** bright, flat colour fields (signal blue, tomato red, off-white), real-feeling phone UI drawn large, Inter / bold grotesk type, with no paper or serif.
- **Sound:** a light pizzicato morning loop that cuts out at the first failure, then returns warmer at the end. UI taps, notification pings, a glitch on each failure, a soft "ding" at the human moment.

## B. Pull the plug

- **Device / form:** the object is the explanation; one frame that rebuilds.
- **What you see and feel:** a giant industrial power strip fills the screen. Each plug is labelled with a hidden AI job (spam filter, fraud check, maps, translation, forecasts). A hand pulls them out one by one, and the strip rebuilds around each consequence. It's tactile, heavy and slightly ominous.
- **First 2 s:** close-up of a chunky plug labelled `SPAM FILTER`. A hand yanks it out with a spark, and the counter behind it jumps to **45.6%**.
- **Beats:** first plug pulled → four more plugs, each pull reveals its effect inside the strip → every light off, silence → a hand plugs a small backup cable labelled `PEOPLE` into the last socket → claim.
- **Look:** black rubber, hazard-yellow stripes, condensed sans type, a hard top light.
- **Sound:** mechanical clunks, electrical hum that drops in pitch with each pull, then full silence before the backup click.

## C. The city map goes quiet (the bold one)

- **Device / form:** a flight through one world.
- **What you see and feel:** a daylight isometric city where every moving thing (traffic flow, parcel routes, card taps, weather balloons) is guided by a tiny blue line of AI. The camera glides across. The blue lines snap off district by district, and the city visibly jams. Then people with clipboards and paper maps restore movement, slower.
- **First 2 s:** wide isometric city at full flow; one blue route line flickers and snaps.
- **Beats:** flow → snap → traffic jam / parcels pile up / inbox tower grows to **45.6%** → people step in → flow resumes, slower → claim.
- **Look:** isometric flat illustration, pastel blocks, one electric-blue accent for AI.
- **Sound:** city ambience, a low drone that snaps silent at the outage, footsteps and chatter as people take over.

## Assumptions

- **Length:** 45–60 s.
- **Format:** 16:9 at 1920×1080, 30 fps, English on screen.
- **Audio:** music and sound effects, no voiceover.
- **Cost:** everything is made locally, free. A HeyGen voice or catalogue music would be paid, and I'm not using either.
