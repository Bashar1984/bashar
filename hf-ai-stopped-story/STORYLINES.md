# What if AI stopped working? Story-driven version (three storylines)

The ask: same subject, told as a real story, with a character the viewer cares about, stakes, a turn and a payoff.
Each storyline is narrated by on-screen story lines (a storyteller's voice in short sentences, no voiceover unless you add one).
Each runs 60–75 s, 16:9 at 1920×1080, English, with music and sound effects.

## A. The interview (recommended: a ticking clock, a choice and a twist)
- **Device / form:** a character does the work, against a clock that never leaves the screen; the camera follows Sam through one morning.
- **What you see and feel:** Sam, a drawn character with a face that reacts, has the most important job interview of her life at 9:00 across the city. At 8:00 every AI helper dies, and each failure costs her minutes. Then a lost tourist asks her for help. She's late, but she stops anyway. The tourist turns out to be the interviewer: "You got me here without a single app. When can you start?"
- **First 2 s:** an alarm clock shows 8:00 above Sam's sleepy face, with the line "Sam had one job today."
- **Beats:** the stakes (interview at 9:00) → the map dies (lost) → the card is declined (no bus ticket, she runs) → the clock reads 8:47, she's out of breath → a tourist asks the way, and translate is dead → her choice: she walks them there, drawing a map by hand → arrival at 9:04, late → the twist: the tourist sits down behind the interview desk → payoff line: "The AI stopped. People didn't."
- **Look:** flat picture-book city in bold colour blocks; Sam drawn with simple shapes (round head, eyes and mouth that change mood); one ticking clock badge that turns red as time runs out.
- **Sound:** a ticking pulse that speeds up as the clock runs down, a glitch on each failure, footsteps when she runs. The music drops out at her choice, then comes back warm at the twist.

## B. The day Grandma took over
- **Device / form:** a memory, "the day the AI stopped", told by a grown-up looking back; picture-book pages turning.
- **What you see and feel:** a kid panics when every app dies. Grandma calmly does it all the old way: a paper map, cash, a phone number she knows by heart, asking neighbours. It's warm and funny, and ends with the grown-up kid teaching their own child to read a paper map.
- **First 2 s:** a page turns, and the line "I was nine the day the AI stopped." appears.
- **Beats:** the memory opens → the apps die and the kid panics → Grandma's first old trick → three more, each a gag → the kid is impressed → time jump to the kid grown up → the lesson passed on.
- **Look:** warm storybook illustration, paper-cut layers, hand-lettered titles.
- **Sound:** a soft piano or plucked strings, page-turn whooshes, a laugh beat on each gag.

## C. The last message (the bold one)
- **Device / form:** told from the AI's point of view: a tiny assistant character living inside a phone, in one continuous take inside the screen.
- **What you see and feel:** the assistant gets the shutdown notice. Its battery-like life bar drains while it watches its human through the camera, worried she won't cope. As it goes dark, it sees her look up and ask a stranger, who helps. Its last words: "She'll be fine."
- **First 2 s:** a cheerful little assistant wakes up inside the phone, and a red banner slides in: "Shutdown in 60 seconds."
- **Beats:** the countdown starts → the assistant panics and tries to help one last time → it watches through the lens → the human struggles → a stranger helps → the assistant smiles and fades → black screen, then the human's world keeps going, in full colour.
- **Look:** glowing UI world inside the phone (warm, not neon) against the real world outside, seen through the lens.
- **Sound:** a gentle music-box loop that slows down as the countdown runs, then the outside world's sounds (street, voices) take over.
